\section{Conclusion}
Using a well established foundational fact-checking framework from~\cite{ullrich-etal-2024-aic,ullrich-drchal-2025-aic}, we introduce a new pipeline for image-text fact-checking using a dual-retrieval multimodal RAG system.
The two retrieval modules our system uses are a text-based similarity search and a reverse image search (RIS) accessed through an API.

Our system scores 3rd place in the \averitec{} shared task, with a combined verdict score of 0.35, a question score of 0.81, an evidence score of 0.35, and a justification score of 0.3, outperforming the baseline across the board.
With this paper, we publish a detailed description of our system design, code and prompts we used, as well as insights into the costs of its deployment and possible points of failure.

\subsection{Future works}
\label{sec:future}
\begin{enumerate}
\item During our exploratory analysis, the RIS engine (Google Lens) often provided zero results for older claims (e.g., 2022 in the dev set) or for images with explicit graphical content. A more robust RIS provider or a fallback strategy could reduce empty evidence sets, which is preferable to having no visual context for the RAG pipeline.
\item The occasional absence of RIS results, combined with the fact that not all gold evidence includes image references, motivates an agentic extension of our pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline2026}). An LLM controller could decide whether to use RIS, text retrieval, or both, saving resources when RIS is unlikely to help.
\item Finally, our findings in section~\ref{sec:bottlenecks} suggest possible discrepancies between how our system represents image-text evidence and how the \averitec{} evaluator expects it. Addressing this mismatch may improve scores on this benchmark and in future shared tasks derived from it.
\end{enumerate}
